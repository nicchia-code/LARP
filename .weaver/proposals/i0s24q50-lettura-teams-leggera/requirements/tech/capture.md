# Capture on Core Audio and AVFoundation

## recorder
- The recorder runs on the main actor and is observable by the menu bar; it hands its start and stop events to a single consumer through an unbounded stream, buffered until iteration starts.
- Call apps using the microphone are detected from the Core Audio process objects: a listener on the process list and one on the running-input property of each process, on a private serial queue; never by polling. The responsible process of a process is read through `responsibility_get_pid_for_pid`, an undocumented function of macOS looked up at run time with `dlsym`; when it is missing or fails, the rules that follow it apply as before. The parent processes are read through the BSD process information, and the bundle identifiers of the responsible and parent processes through the running applications of AppKit. The reading of process information (responsible process, parent, bundle identifier) sits behind a protocol, replaced in tests.
- The detection writes to the diagnostic log of LARP, area `detection`, a line every time a process starts or stops using the microphone: its Core Audio process object, process identifier, bundle identifier as Core Audio reports it (or empty), the responsible process with its bundle identifier, the chain of parent processes with the process identifier and bundle identifier of each level and why the walk stopped, and the call app recognised or none. The processes using the microphone are kept, with what was read about them, on the private queue of the detection, so that the stop of a process is written with the same details.
- The microphone permission goes through `AVAudioApplication`. The system audio permission is probed by creating and immediately destroying a private global process tap that excludes the app's own process.
- Proposals, warnings and notices are borderless non-activating panels at the status bar level, on every Space and beside full-screen apps, placed under the menu bar icon; alerts are modal and activate the app first.
- Detection, permissions, the proposal, the alerts and every notice sit behind protocols, replaced in tests.
- The global shortcuts are registered with `RegisterEventHotKey` of the Carbon framework, which needs no Accessibility permission; they are registered again whenever they change and unregistered when removed; the registration sits behind a protocol, replaced in tests.

## recording-view
- The pill is a borderless non-activating panel at the floating level, on every Space and beside full-screen apps, placed on the screen of the menu bar; the live window is a normal window that comes to the front when opened, without making the app appear in the Dock.
- Levels are measured on the private queue of each track, from the 16 kHz audio before it is written, and handed to the main actor at most 20 times a second; nothing is measured on the audio threads.
- The text being recognised comes from the volatile results of the live speech transcriber, which are passed to the live window and replaced by each new result; only final results become lines of the call.

## capture
- One capture per recording owns the two track writers, the echo remover, the microphone and the system audio. It starts the system audio first and the microphone second, and stops the microphone, then the system audio, then waits for the echo remover and the two tracks to finish.
- What the capture tells the recorder may be reported from any thread and is handed to the main actor.
- Audio threads only copy the buffer they get and return. Conversion to the storage format, echo cancellation, disk writes and the handing over to the live transcription run on private serial queues, one per track and one for the echo remover; every buffer keeps the format it was captured with and the system uptime of its last frame.
- Sample rate conversion uses one `AVAudioConverter` per stream, kept between buffers and rebuilt when the input format changes.
- The AAC files are written with `ExtAudioFile`, and the packet table is written out with `AudioFileOptimize`, because `AVAudioFile` keeps it in memory until the file is closed and an unclosed file could not be read at all.
- The microphone and the system audio sources are made by factories that tests replace.
- A diagnostic log goes through `os.Logger`, with the app's subsystem and the category `capture`, stays on the Mac and never holds audio or transcript text: at every start and restart of the system audio, the format the tap reports and the format of the stream actually read; at every start and restart of the microphone, the format tapped; at the stop of a recording, for each track, the seconds of audio written, the seconds of silence added and the length of the recording; and, for each source, how many buffers were dropped and why (a run already ended or no longer following the output, a buffer that could not be read, a conversion that failed).
- The same lines are also written to the diagnostic log of LARP, area `capture`, together with: the reason of every restart of the microphone (microphone to use changed, format of the microphone changed, microphone gone, microphone chosen by the user) and every new echo canceller made because only the output changed; the reason of every restart of the system audio (which value of its signature changed, or how many seconds without audio when it stalled); every loss and every return of the microphone and of the system audio. The lines of `os.Logger` stay as they are. Nothing is written from an audio thread: the lines are written from the private queues.

## microphone
- Built on Core Audio, input only: an audio unit `kAudioUnitSubType_HALOutput` with input enabled and output disabled, its current device set to the microphone to use (the chosen input device, otherwise the default input device), started at every start and restart. The format is read from the input side of the device at every start; a sample rate of zero or no channels means there is no microphone. The input callback renders into a buffer prepared in advance, copies it and returns. `AVAudioEngine` is not used for the microphone, because its output side stops it at every change of the output.
- macOS voice processing is never enabled: it would lower the audio of every other app and could change the level of the microphone.
- Changes are followed through Core Audio listeners, on a private queue and removed outside the lock of the microphone: on the default input device and on the device list, so that the chosen device is picked up again when it is connected; on the sample rate, the stream format and the liveness of the device in use, which restart the capture; and on the default output and its sample rate, which only make a new echo canceller. A new choice of microphone during a recording restarts the capture as a change of devices does.
- The echo canceller is WebRTC's AEC3 alone, from the local package built from source, reached through its thin C wrapper (create, destroy, process one reference frame, process one microphone frame); mobile mode is off. It is not thread-safe and is used only from the queue of the echo remover.
- The input unit and its listeners sit behind a protocol, replaced in tests by fake devices.

## system-audio
- Built on a Core Audio process tap: a mono global tap of every process except the app itself, not muted, private; read through a private, non-stacked aggregate device whose main and only sub-device is the current default output, with the tap started automatically and drift compensation on, and an I/O proc on its own queue.
- The format that labels every buffer of a run is read after the aggregate device has started: it is the format of the tap's own stream among the input streams of the aggregate device, at the sample rate the aggregate device really runs at, never the format the tap reported before the aggregate device was built.
- The I/O proc reads only the buffer of the tap's stream, found by its place among the input streams of the aggregate device, never by assuming it is the first one: a headset in its call mode adds its own microphone stream to the aggregate device. When the tap's stream cannot be found, the run does not start, as when the tap cannot be created.
- Buffers arriving after a run has ended or stopped following the output are dropped.
- A run listens to the default output, the sample rate and liveness of the output, the sample rate and active sub-devices of the aggregate device, the format of the tap and the format of the tap's stream in the aggregate device, on a private queue never on the I/O thread, and asks for a restart when a signature of those values differs from the one taken at its start, or when the format of the tap's stream differs from the format that labels the buffers of the run. A first check runs right after the start, for what changed while it was being set up, such as headphones switching to their call mode when the microphone opens.
- A watch on the private queue of the run, never on the I/O thread, notices when the I/O proc has delivered no buffer for 2 seconds and asks for a restart, like a change of devices; when the restart fails the system audio is lost.
- Starting and stopping run on one private serial queue, one thing at a time; while the system audio is lost, listeners on the default output and on the device list stand in for the run.
- A failure to create the tap is what macOS does when the system audio recording permission is missing.
- The Core Audio access sits behind a protocol, replaced in tests by fake devices.
