# LARP

A macOS menu bar app for Apple silicon Macs that records the user's calls and transcribes them on the Mac. It notices when a watched call app, Microsoft Teams first of all, starts using the microphone and offers to record, or records on its own when the user wants it; the user can also record by hand, from the menu bar or with a global keyboard shortcut. It records the microphone the user chose (the user's voice, with the sound of the others removed) and the system audio (the other participants) as two tracks, transcribes them in Italian or English with the user's voice as "Io" and everybody else as "Altri", then tells the other participants apart by their voices. A recording can be paused; while it runs, a small pill floats on the screen and a live window can show the transcript as it is written, with bookmarks and notes the user adds.

When the user allows it, the app also reads, on the Mac, the calendar of the Mac and Microsoft Teams through the Accessibility of macOS: the title of the meeting in progress becomes the title of the call, the people invited or present are offered as names for the participants, and who Teams shows as speaking names the voices of the others, live and after the call. None of this leaves the Mac or reaches omp.

When the user turns them on, two optional features send the text of the transcript, never the audio, to omp, an assistant program the user installs and configures on the Mac: the refinement of the transcript, and a summary of each call with its key concepts, decisions and next steps. Both are off by default and independent of each other.

At the first launch a welcome window asks for the permissions and the first choices. A click on the menu bar icon opens a small dark popover with the state of the recorder and the way to record, to open the archive and the settings. The main window has a dark sidebar and shows one page at a time: the library of the recorded and imported calls as a grid of cards with their waveforms, which the user searches, filters by call app and groups into folders, and the list of every bookmark; the page of one call, with its summary, notes and bookmarks beside its transcript and the audio that plays following the transcript at the speed chosen, where the user finds words in the transcript and corrects it, renames the call and its participants, sees how long each one spoke, copies or exports the call as Markdown and deletes it; and the settings, with the detection of calls, the microphone, the transcription language, the two omp switches with whether omp can be reached, the keyboard shortcuts and starting at login.

Audio always stays on the Mac; the app has no cloud service, account or telemetry of its own. The interface follows one visual language throughout, and is in Italian when the Mac is in Italian, in English otherwise.
